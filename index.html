Vortex extension · Nexus Mods

Event Horizon

Build a collection that arrives the way you built it. Then keep it that way.

A Vortex Collection is a list of mods: here they are, install them. An Event Horizon package is a snapshot of a setup that worked: rebuild it exactly, and prove it matches.

1,900 mods
in the Skyrim SE profile it is proven on, plus a 950-mod Fallout 4 collection
17,159 of 17,161
contested files go to the curator's winner on a 1,753-mod collection; the other two are files the game never loads
2,600+ tests
including a render of every screen

For players

  • The whole plan before anything is written, with your game setup checked first.
  • One mod at a time, every archive hashed against the manifest.
  • The curator's load order, pinned and watched: you're told the moment a sort undoes it.
  • Collection Doctor shows what is still true, with the repair on each finding.

For curators

  • Your whole profile in one table: updates, missing requirements, duplicates and plugins.
  • "Make it work" reads a mod's whole requirement chain and installs it in dependency order.
  • Builds tell a gone file from a gone page, and ship what can't be downloaded any more by hash.

Skyrim Special Edition, Fallout 4, Fallout 3, Fallout: New Vegas and Starfield, on Vortex 2. Windows, plus Linux and Steam Deck through Proton. Built by DuduPhudu and Bluuuk; free on Nexus Mods, source available under PolyForm Strict.

AI coding agents · npm

bearing

Your tests catch code that breaks. Nothing catches an agent that has drifted about what the code means.

  • North-stars outrank every other doc, so an idea you killed can't quietly come back.
  • Task-core saves the task before compaction throws the detail away.
  • Microscope reviews like an expert in your domain, and every finding must survive a refutation pass.
  • Eleven independent modules for Claude Code, Zed and Codex, at about 25,000 tokens a session.

Transcription · CLI

whiz

From a recording to a labeled, named, frame-illustrated transcript in one command.

  • Knows your speakers: name someone once, and later recordings label them automatically.
  • Sees the screen: the analysis checks what was shown against what was said.
  • Made for hour-long videos, with an Essentials summary you can feed back later.
  • Local and private by default, on whisper.cpp and Ollama: no uploads, no API keys.

Omarchy plugin · face unlock

PenguID

Look at your laptop and you're in.

The infrared camera recognizes you on the lock screen, at sudo and in polkit prompts, while your password stays the real key. Outside the lock screen, a small penguin card shows each step above every window, like Face ID on an iPhone.

$ sudo pacman -Syu Press Enter for face, or type your password:
  • Face data stays on the laptop, encrypted under a key sealed by the TPM.
  • Face ID-style rules: it asks for your password after boot, after 48 hours without it, and after 5 refused faces.
  • It fails closed: if a check can't run, you simply get the password prompt.

Voice dictation · macOS and Linux

mynah

Say it. It types where you are.

Press a key and talk, and the words land in whatever window has focus — a commit message, a chat, a form. Named after the bird that says back what it hears.

you say the fee is computed · on gross,
it types while you are still talking, at each pause
  • Text arrives in the gaps, not at the end: every pause closes an utterance and types it.
  • Local by definition: speech is transcribed on your own hardware, with no account, no API key and nothing to send.
  • When a sentence gets cut in half, a test says so — the segmentation thresholds are pinned by a corpus of recordings.
  • On Omarchy it has a face: the bird sits in the bar with your level beside it, and a pill shows what it heard.
